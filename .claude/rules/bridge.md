# The app-clock bridge (dlink::bridge: Fifo, Player, AppBuffer)

- **The target is a fixed device term** (`app_buffer_ns`, #247, owner 13.11.2026): the app's chunk + one pump block at the device rate, or the configured value verbatim. Never measured, never grown after a dropout. A dropout is counted (`Stats::underruns`) and re-primes to the same target. Do not bring back a cushion or a fixed constant. Its range is `MIN_APP_BUFFER_NS` (1 ms) to `MAX_APP_BUFFER_NS` for every dlink app; a user with a measured floor of its own sets it with `AppBuffer::with_floor` (#422: dstack-asio's per-direction targets, 0.5 ms), and `problem`, `problem_for(key)` and the clamp (`clamped_ns`, what a player plays) follow it. Never lower `MIN_APP_BUFFER_NS` for one consumer's shape.
- **Chunked producer vs chunked consumer:** the depth is a sawtooth of the wider swing (dsend's 10 ms packets, OBS's 1024-frame ticks on the producer side; dlisten's 10 ms output callback on the consumer side). Priming adds half the wider swing (`max(callback_in, chunk_in)`), so the MEAN sits at the target. Without it a producer-chunked bridge at a target of chunk + block starts at the sawtooth's top and drops out: a 48 kHz capture on a 96 kHz network dropped out every 20 ms (#247).
- **No floor; the excess follows the target** (#303, v0.6.140): a deficit is a dropout only when the FIFO cannot serve the converter's next call (`pop_planar` fails -> `underrun`, re-primed to the same target); the controller gets a floor of 0. The old floor (min(5 ms, target/4)) re-primed a writer late inside its app's pass (OBS's NDI output ahead of the TX tap, ~10 ms) while the FIFO still held audio: ~22 ms of silence, `tx_underruns` = `tx_recentres` at ~0.8/s on stream-snv. Do not bring back a floor. Hard excess = max(2 x target, 2 x chunk); a 2 x target excess alone cut the small default targets.
- **Drift costs depth at the start:** the ASRC correction slews 5 ppm/s (`asrc::MAX_SLEW_PPM_PER_S`), so a clock 200 ppm off costs about 4 to 6 ms of depth over its first ~40 s. At the default (10 ms + block) ±100 ppm holds; 200 ppm drops out once or twice on one sign. The simulated-clock tests pin ±100 ppm at the default and ±200 ppm at a set 20 ms; never "fix" them by loosening the asserts.
- **A test whose bridge carries a burst** (a 49 ms block in one call, a 100 ms push) needs a target whose excess bound covers it (a set 40 ms), or the player skips the burst as an excess by design.
- **`recentres` counts excess skips only** (since v0.6.140, #303; before, a floor deficit counted in both `underruns` and `recentres`). A short target (under chunk/2 + block, `AppBuffer::short`) runs dry every chunk (an underrun); test the excess bound with the pure `hard_excess_s` or a burst past it.
- **An app on its own clock stamps by `Block::play_at()`** (#295, v0.6.132): the time the app PLAYS the block, its media start + P + (c) in the app's clock; since #476 the pump hands it H = 30 % of P earlier (`stream::handoff_lead`, `Timing::leads`, the device config's `rx_handoff_lead`), never at `play_at` (before, every block came after it by the wake delay). A pulled RX (`RxPull`) has no lead (`Embedded::launch` clears it) (`clock::app_monotonic_ns`: Linux CLOCK_MONOTONIC, Windows RAW QPC ns, not the steered `clock::monotonic_ns`). Stamping "first arrival + sample count" drifts by the media-vs-local rate (~6 ppm = ~21 ms/h on stream-snv) and forced obs's hourly re-anchor step. The pair is read once per pump wake; P + B on the sample grid can read 1 ns above dlink's logged RX path (15 000 000 vs 14 999 999 ns), so compare with a 1 ns tolerance.
- **A player in a dlink `Process` calls `fill_at(block.start(), ..)`** (#303): a start that is not the last one's end (the pump's rebootstrap) resyncs once (`Stats::resyncs`, status `resyncs`); `fill_with` never does. A block filled in pieces passes each piece's start (`block.start() + done`), or every piece after the first resyncs (dsend, dstack-asio). An output callback (dlisten) names no start: `fill_with`, or `fill_pulled` on a fill-the-room output.
- **A chunked producer's re-prime** (#303): the player follows the producer's schedule (`player/writer.rs`, a line from frames written to time) and re-primes to the depth whose mean over the writer's cycle is the target (`phased_level`). Before, a re-prime ending at a stall's catch-up burst left the mean up to a chunk over the target and the braking ramped to -70 ppm. Rounding the next write's distance to whole calls is right only for a jitter-free writer: it measured worse in the soak (OBS jitters ±2 ms), so a test producer for this path jitters too (`player/resync_tests.rs`). A capture that stops and restarts (dsend) moves the schedule only after two writes off it by the same error; while it is overdue the player primes as before.
- **The producer's chunk is its own `Player::new` argument** (#317, v0.6.144): what the FIFO's writer pushes at once, never the target's chunk by default. The app's tick for a FIFO the app writes (OBS TX, `buffer.chunk_ns()`), the capture packet (dsend), the ASIO buffer (dstack-asio from ASIO), the pump's block for a FIFO the pump writes (dstack-asio to ASIO, `buffer.block_ns()`), the output's period for a FIFO its callback pulls (dlisten since #307, `buffer.chunk_ns()`). The to-ASIO player once took the ASIO buffer (1115 frames at 96 kHz) for a pump writing 32: every re-prime followed a schedule that does not exist, and after a pump skip it re-primed under one callback's take on every callback (86 underruns/s for minutes). The phased re-prime never starts under one output call + the converter's need (`phased_start`); a floor at the target would undo the sawtooth's mean (#303), and `the_phased_level_puts_the_mean_on_the_target` pins it. A writer's count up by a chunk and a quarter more is no burst: a reader that looks once a callback sees a small writer's whole period at once. The pump-skip simulation is `dstack-asio/src/bridge/tests/pump_skip.rs` (the field's 32-frame pump, 512 @ 44.1 kHz, a 38-block skip). The priming swing is `max(callback_in, chunk_in)` with `callback_in` the frames of ONE fill call: a side that fills a callback in pieces primes for a piece (half a 512 buffer: +100 ppm within 30 s at no drift), so dstack-asio's driver side fills its whole buffer at once (scratch sized at the open); `the_to_asio_player_primes_for_the_whole_asio_buffer` pins it.
- **An app's status hook runs once a second, read or not** (#317, `daemon::media::app`): it used to run only while the status was read within 5 s, so dasio's 5-minute status log carried the previous log's part, stale (`to_asio_underruns: 0` mid-storm). `stale` now means only a hook that has not returned for 3 s.
- **A caller that names its starts is steered on the producer's line** (#303): `Fifo::push_with` stamps each write on the FIFO's clock (`Fifo::with_clock`; default `clock::app_monotonic_ns`, a seqlock of four words, no lock or allocation), and `Player::steer` measures depth and rate point against the line through the writes: frames at the last write + (now - its stamp) x nominal rate, clamped to [0, one mean write], less half the mean write. The writer's schedule (`player/writer.rs`) takes the stamps too, and a re-prime on the line starts half a call more than the sampled one (a whole call over the level: its first observation follows the call's take); without it a resync left ~1.2 ms under the target and the braking pinned the bound. A stamp read that meets a write in progress on every try skips that one observation (never the sampled depth in its place: up to half a write off the line). The mean write stands for the producer's cycle: one push a cycle (OBS's tick, dsend's packet, an ASIO callback); a cycle pushed in pieces back to back would halve it. `fill_with` and `fill_pulled` (dlisten's callback) keep the sampled depth and read no clock (every push still stamps, one clock read). **Aliasing lesson:** the sampled depth (after each 128-frame converter call) against OBS's 1024-frame writes is a commensurate grid; with a jitter-free writer the samples' phase stays fixed until the write instant drifts across a call (~444 s at 6 ppm), so the window mean and rate point held still, then stepped 2.67 ms and the braking pinned the ±20 bound (obs's soak on v0.6.140). Any writer jitter (the old gate's ±2 ms) dithers it away and hides it: keep a steady-writer case (`Writer::Steady`) in the gate. A test that drives a player with `fill_at(Some(..))` on simulated time must give its FIFO the same simulated clock (`with_clock`), or the line reads wall time.
- **The level loop is bounded at ±20 ppm around the locked rate** (`LEVEL_BOUND_PPM`, `asrc::Controller::with_level_bound_ppm`, the main's decision on #303), only once settled (fit locked and a window in the calm zone) and while the fit stays locked: before that the braking itself follows the drift (200 ppm off holds about 5 ms of offset at the lock), and bounding it then broke the ±200 ppm tests (250 s to drain).
- **The soak** (`dlink/tests/soak.rs` + `soak/harness.rs`, the release gate, #304) is where a Player change is proven: 14 `gate_*` cases (incl. the late audio pass and the steady writer, #303; three on the pulled TX path in `soak/pulled.rs`, `Tx::Pulled`/`PulledTxStall`, judged on the fill's own counts too, #363); the harness drives the TX FIFO's clock with the simulated app time (`simulated_path`, `Ends::set_app_ns`) and checks that every TX tone break from the TX bridge's first primed step (settle time included, never only the judged window) has its own counted underrun, re-centre or resync at most 20 ms before it (`unaccounted`, #303), about 3 min of wall time in parallel on a loaded dev1. A converter error is counted too (`Stats::convert_errors`, status `convert_errors`). A one-off trace of the controller (rate, braking, level, applied per window) is the fastest way to a cause there; the rate fit held +6.0 ppm through every stall, the ramp was the braking on a real offset.
- **A consumer with its own deadline pulls** (#307, `bridge::pull::RxPull`, `Link::start_pulling`): the output callback drains the flow sockets and reads the rings by position (media now less P, each channel at its delay) into the FIFO in ONE push (pieces back to back would halve the player's mean write), so a late pump never empties it. The pull is the rings' only writer: pull from the device's start (`tests/rx_pull.rs` once started its output after `Receiving`: nothing drained, no keepalive went, the flow was closed as silent and asked again, 157735 samples off), and while the consumer stops, another thread calls `drain_idle` (dlisten's main thread, every 250 ms while `PullStats::pulls` stands still, decided by `IdleWatch`; it empties the sockets, counted `unplaced`, so the backlog never grows past a look). Under the app's writer `late` is judged against P + the time since the last drain (`intake::reads_apart`), not P + the pump's block (on dlisten's small P most packets read late otherwise). Latency is the mean of the FIFO's top (after the pull) and foot (after the fill): pulled, that is the whole wait from due time to playing; pump-written, add half a pump block (dev1 at 12.667 ms: 14.34 pulled vs 14.96 pumped). A consumer shares its pull through `bridge::SharedPull` (the never-waiting `pull`, the idle `keep`, `clear_poison`; dlisten and dstack-asio), never a copy of that logic. Prove a pull change with `tests/rx_pull.rs` (pump stalls 10-20 ms against a steady 10 ms output: 0 underruns, 0 off, 0 late, the wait bound), `tests/rx_pull_alloc.rs` and the rtsan job's `-p dlink --test rtsan`.
- **A producer on its own clock is pulled by the TX thread** (#363, `bridge::TxPull`, `Link::start_tx_pulling`): the TX twin of #307. The pull is the player (+ `Switch`) that dsend's processor was; the device's TX thread runs it in whole blocks at the pump's due time and stamp, just before it sends, so a late pump never sends silence on its channels. Its output rate is the app's: on a converting device the start wraps it in `Rated` with 0 inputs (the same TX converter and prime as the pump path: (c) TX unchanged). A TX computed from RX in the same block stays on the pump (`Link::start`). dsend uses it; obs may switch; dstack-asio's from-ASIO side uses it since #364 (`FromAsio`, a `bridge::TxProcess` wrapping the pull with its block age's take: `start_tx_pulling` takes any `TxProcess`, `Link::start_pulling_with_tx` pulls RX and TX on one device).
- **An output callback held past its buffer** (#307, 17.11.2026, mbc's dlisten bursts): a fill-the-room output (WASAPI shared via cpal: each call is the buffer less its padding) whose engine reads a period at each tick comes back from a hold with a pull of the whole time held and a call of the whole buffer (a dry call). `Player::fill_pulled` + `player/gap.rs`: the silence of a dry call can only be `p - B mod p` (mod p) plus whole periods (B the buffer = the largest call, p the usual call = the smallest that came twice); the excess is that silence plus the call's phase off the grid less the call-before's lateness (`Lateness`: measured against the least of the 4 calls before; `forget` after a gap, whose calls carry its silence: a stale floor read a hold 2 calls after a gap 3 ms late and dropped a period too many; never clamped at 0, a pull-0 call before the hold reads a call under); at the dry call drop the lattice point the excess surely holds (`snap` slack 0: never over), then after `SETTLE_CALLS` calls `snap` the lowest running sum plus that lateness (slack a quarter period: up to 3/4 period of late wakes after the hold) and drop the rest. A big call short of B is no gap. Four designs were refuted by fresh reviews before this one, each with a RED or a unit row: the whole excess also drops the phase; whole periods break on Windows' real buffer (1056 frames for 480) and on a late wake after the hold; the lowest sum alone keeps the settling calls' least lateness (a burst of late wakes after a hold was dropped as silence: 6866 for 4224, underruns); and dropping only the excess past a period at the dry call left the FIFO a period over, which with a late call before the hold crossed the excess bound (re-centre, underrun). A fixed-size ASIO switch never opens a gap, and dlisten uses `fill_pulled` on Windows only (ALSA through PipeWire calls follow the hardware position). Not covered: a hold after the callback read its room but before its pull. The time goes to the output's clock (`skipped`, converted from the whole loss so per-drop rounding does not add up). Do NOT reset the converter there: a reset rings from silence (~134 samples off the level a gap). Simulate such an output with an engine that reads the buffer tick by tick on its own clock (a period or what is left, the rest silence), a 1056-frame buffer, events on those ticks (the missed ones gone), late wakes after the hold past the settling, never a thread that catches up back to back (an ASIO-shaped caller; the excess skip then underruns by design, see `to_asio_pull.rs`). `tests/rx_pull_held.rs` asserts the drop equal to the card's silence to the frame (its window ends outside a gap: a gap's drop and its silence are counted a few calls apart) and judges the level per callback, leaving out a callback whose read met a ring hole and the one after it (the sender's; at dev1 load 7-15 a plain run can show such holes). The pull itself caught up every time (restarts 0): look at the player before the pull.
  - **A dry call less late than the floor** (#433, v0.6.187 tag CI on windows-2025: 1 underrun, 1 re-centre, `output_lost` = `card_silence` - 480, 1164 off, no ring hole): the excess is the silence + the dry call's lateness past its tick - the floor's lateness past theirs, and the floor is relative, so where every wake is late (the runner's ~1 ms timer) a hold that ended less late past its tick drops a period under at the dry call. In the CI run that was most likely the 12th, unscheduled gap (a runner stall that ended under 1 ms past a tick: 5568 = 11 x 384 + 1344, 5088 = 11 x 384 + 864); the scheduled 25 ms hold on a 1 ms late timer ends ~6 ms past its tick, over the floor, and drops the silence exactly. That period sat in the FIFO into the big call; in `rx_pull_held`'s shape the depth was then within a block of the excess bound (target 12.667 + hard excess 25.333 ms = 1824 frames) and crossed it in the converter's latest phase (480 mod 128 = 96: four phases a call; the test's holds every 70 events met two). Since #433 `Player::steer` makes no observation while a gap is open (dry call through settling): the owed period is the gap's, never an excess. A callback held on every call keeps its gap open and the controller unobserved (the converter keeps its last ratio); the gap's running sum still drops the silence, so the depth holds (`a_callback_held_on_every_call_keeps_the_depth`, 40 dry calls). A gap-path change is proven with `rx_pull_held`'s late-timer case (every wake 1 ms late, the hold 0.9 ms past a tick; before the fix 4 of 4 under `chrt -f 10`, 3 underruns each; 0.3 ms past did not cross) and its unit row; dev1's own wakes (~56 us late) never reach this case.
