# The app-clock bridge (dlink::bridge: Fifo, Player, AppBuffer)

- **The target is a fixed device term** (`app_buffer_ns`, #247, owner 13.11.2026): the app's chunk + one pump block at the device rate, or the configured value verbatim. Never measured, never grown after a dropout. A dropout is counted (`Stats::underruns`) and re-primes to the same target. Do not bring back a cushion or a fixed constant.
- **Chunked producer vs chunked consumer:** the depth is a sawtooth of the wider swing (dsend's 10 ms packets, OBS's 1024-frame ticks on the producer side; dlisten's 10 ms output callback on the consumer side). Priming adds half the wider swing (`max(callback_in, chunk_in)`), so the MEAN sits at the target. Without it a producer-chunked bridge at a target of chunk + block starts at the sawtooth's top and drops out: a 48 kHz capture on a 96 kHz network dropped out every 20 ms (#247).
- **No floor; the excess follows the target** (#303, v0.6.140): a deficit is a dropout only when the FIFO cannot serve the converter's next call (`pop_planar` fails -> `underrun`, re-primed to the same target); the controller gets a floor of 0. The old floor (min(5 ms, target/4)) re-primed a writer late inside its app's pass (OBS's NDI output ahead of the TX tap, ~10 ms) while the FIFO still held audio: ~22 ms of silence, `tx_underruns` = `tx_recentres` at ~0.8/s on stream-snv. Do not bring back a floor. Hard excess = max(2 x target, 2 x chunk); a 2 x target excess alone cut the small default targets.
- **Drift costs depth at the start:** the ASRC correction slews 5 ppm/s (`asrc::MAX_SLEW_PPM_PER_S`), so a clock 200 ppm off costs about 4 to 6 ms of depth over its first ~40 s. At the default (10 ms + block) ±100 ppm holds; 200 ppm drops out once or twice on one sign. The simulated-clock tests pin ±100 ppm at the default and ±200 ppm at a set 20 ms; never "fix" them by loosening the asserts.
- **A test whose bridge carries a burst** (a 49 ms block in one call, a 100 ms push) needs a target whose excess bound covers it (a set 40 ms), or the player skips the burst as an excess by design.
- **`recentres` counts excess skips only** (since v0.6.140, #303; before, a floor deficit counted in both `underruns` and `recentres`). A short target (under chunk/2 + block, `AppBuffer::short`) runs dry every chunk (an underrun); test the excess bound with the pure `hard_excess_s` or a burst past it.
- **An app on its own clock stamps by `Block::play_at()`** (#295, v0.6.132): the block's media start + P + (c) in the app's clock (`clock::app_monotonic_ns`: Linux CLOCK_MONOTONIC, Windows RAW QPC ns, not the steered `clock::monotonic_ns`). Stamping "first arrival + sample count" drifts by the media-vs-local rate (~6 ppm = ~21 ms/h on stream-snv) and forced obs's hourly re-anchor step. The pair is read once per pump wake; P + B on the sample grid can read 1 ns above dlink's logged RX path (15 000 000 vs 14 999 999 ns), so compare with a 1 ns tolerance.
- **A player in a dlink `Process` calls `fill_at(block.start(), ..)`** (#303): a start that is not the last one's end (the pump's rebootstrap) resyncs once (`Stats::resyncs`, status `resyncs`); `fill_with` never does. A block filled in pieces passes each piece's start (`block.start() + done`), or every piece after the first resyncs (dsend, dstack-asio). An output callback (dlisten) keeps `fill_with`.
- **A chunked producer's re-prime** (#303): the player follows the producer's schedule (`player/writer.rs`, a line from frames written to time) and re-primes to the depth whose mean over the writer's cycle is the target (`phased_level`). Before, a re-prime ending at a stall's catch-up burst left the mean up to a chunk over the target and the braking ramped to -70 ppm. Rounding the next write's distance to whole calls is right only for a jitter-free writer: it measured worse in the soak (OBS jitters ±2 ms), so a test producer for this path jitters too (`player/resync_tests.rs`). A capture that stops and restarts (dsend) moves the schedule only after two writes off it by the same error; while it is overdue the player primes as before.
- **The producer's chunk is its own `Player::new` argument** (#317, v0.6.144): what the FIFO's writer pushes at once, never the target's chunk by default. The app's tick for a FIFO the app writes (OBS TX, `buffer.chunk_ns()`), the capture packet (dsend), the ASIO buffer (dstack-asio from ASIO), the pump's block for a FIFO the pump writes (dstack-asio to ASIO, `buffer.block_ns()`), the output's period for a FIFO its callback pulls (dlisten since #307, `buffer.chunk_ns()`). The to-ASIO player once took the ASIO buffer (1115 frames at 96 kHz) for a pump writing 32: every re-prime followed a schedule that does not exist, and after a pump skip it re-primed under one callback's take on every callback (86 underruns/s for minutes). The phased re-prime never starts under one output call + the converter's need (`phased_start`); a floor at the target would undo the sawtooth's mean (#303), and `the_phased_level_puts_the_mean_on_the_target` pins it. A writer's count up by a chunk and a quarter more is no burst: a reader that looks once a callback sees a small writer's whole period at once. The pump-skip simulation is `dstack-asio/src/bridge/tests/pump_skip.rs` (the field's 32-frame pump, 512 @ 44.1 kHz, a 38-block skip). The priming swing is `max(callback_in, chunk_in)` with `callback_in` the frames of ONE fill call: a side that fills a callback in pieces primes for a piece (half a 512 buffer: +100 ppm within 30 s at no drift), so dstack-asio's driver side fills its whole buffer at once (scratch sized at the open); `the_to_asio_player_primes_for_the_whole_asio_buffer` pins it.
- **An app's status hook runs once a second, read or not** (#317, `daemon::media::app`): it used to run only while the status was read within 5 s, so dasio's 5-minute status log carried the previous log's part, stale (`to_asio_underruns: 0` mid-storm). `stale` now means only a hook that has not returned for 3 s.
- **A caller that names its starts is steered on the producer's line** (#303): `Fifo::push_with` stamps each write on the FIFO's clock (`Fifo::with_clock`; default `clock::app_monotonic_ns`, a seqlock of four words, no lock or allocation), and `Player::steer` measures depth and rate point against the line through the writes: frames at the last write + (now - its stamp) x nominal rate, clamped to [0, one mean write], less half the mean write. The writer's schedule (`player/writer.rs`) takes the stamps too, and a re-prime on the line starts half a call more than the sampled one (a whole call over the level: its first observation follows the call's take); without it a resync left ~1.2 ms under the target and the braking pinned the bound. A stamp read that meets a write in progress on every try skips that one observation (never the sampled depth in its place: up to half a write off the line). The mean write stands for the producer's cycle: one push a cycle (OBS's tick, dsend's packet, an ASIO callback); a cycle pushed in pieces back to back would halve it. `fill_with` (dlisten's callback) keeps the sampled depth and its reader reads no clock (every push still stamps, one clock read). **Aliasing lesson:** the sampled depth (after each 128-frame converter call) against OBS's 1024-frame writes is a commensurate grid; with a jitter-free writer the samples' phase stays fixed until the write instant drifts across a call (~444 s at 6 ppm), so the window mean and rate point held still, then stepped 2.67 ms and the braking pinned the ±20 bound (obs's soak on v0.6.140). Any writer jitter (the old gate's ±2 ms) dithers it away and hides it: keep a steady-writer case (`Writer::Steady`) in the gate. A test that drives a player with `fill_at(Some(..))` on simulated time must give its FIFO the same simulated clock (`with_clock`), or the line reads wall time.
- **The level loop is bounded at ±20 ppm around the locked rate** (`LEVEL_BOUND_PPM`, `asrc::Controller::with_level_bound_ppm`, the main's decision on #303), only once settled (fit locked and a window in the calm zone) and while the fit stays locked: before that the braking itself follows the drift (200 ppm off holds about 5 ms of offset at the lock), and bounding it then broke the ±200 ppm tests (250 s to drain).
- **The soak** (`dlink/tests/soak.rs` + `soak/harness.rs`, the release gate, #304) is where a Player change is proven: 11 `gate_*` cases (incl. the late audio pass and the steady writer, #303); the harness drives the TX FIFO's clock with the simulated app time (`simulated_path`, `Ends::set_app_ns`) and checks that every TX tone break from the TX bridge's first primed step (settle time included, never only the judged window) has its own counted underrun, re-centre or resync at most 20 ms before it (`unaccounted`, #303), about 3 min of wall time in parallel on a loaded dev1. A converter error is counted too (`Stats::convert_errors`, status `convert_errors`). A one-off trace of the controller (rate, braking, level, applied per window) is the fastest way to a cause there; the rate fit held +6.0 ppm through every stall, the ramp was the braking on a real offset.
- **A consumer with its own deadline pulls** (#307, `bridge::pull::RxPull`, `Link::start_pulling`): the output callback drains the flow sockets and reads the rings by position (media now less P, each channel at its delay) into the FIFO in ONE push (pieces back to back would halve the player's mean write), so a late pump never empties it. The pull is the rings' only writer: pull from the device's start (`tests/rx_pull.rs` once started its output after `Receiving`: nothing drained, no keepalive went, the flow was closed as silent and asked again, 157735 samples off), and while the consumer stops, another thread calls `drain_idle` (dlisten's main thread, every 250 ms while `PullStats::pulls` stands still, decided by `IdleWatch`; it empties the sockets, counted `unplaced`, so the backlog never grows past a look). Under the app's writer `late` is judged against P + the time since the last drain (`intake::reads_apart`), not P + the pump's block (on dlisten's small P most packets read late otherwise). Latency is the mean of the FIFO's top (after the pull) and foot (after the fill): pulled, that is the whole wait from due time to playing; pump-written, add half a pump block (dev1 at 12.667 ms: 14.34 pulled vs 14.96 pumped). A consumer shares its pull through `bridge::SharedPull` (the never-waiting `pull`, the idle `keep`, `clear_poison`; dlisten and dstack-asio), never a copy of that logic. Prove a pull change with `tests/rx_pull.rs` (pump stalls 10-20 ms against a steady 10 ms output: 0 underruns, 0 off, 0 late, the wait bound), `tests/rx_pull_alloc.rs` and the rtsan job's `-p dlink --test rtsan`.
