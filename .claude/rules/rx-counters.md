# RX counters, the ring and the minute lines

- **A ring's state reset from the control side races the RX thread** (#234, `Ring::restart`). `RxShared::publish` runs on the control thread. The RX thread keeps its old routes and plan until its next `Receiver::refresh`, so it can still write an old flow's packet after the publish's reset. Reset from the writer too: `refresh`/`retarget` restart before the pass writes. The writer only lowers the state, with `fetch_min`, never a compare-then-store (that leaves an unkillable `<`/`<=` mutant).
- **A per-flow minute rise is per ring, never a per-slot sum of lifetime counters.** A channel routed into a slot brings its whole history into the slot's sum. `RxMinute::holes_rose` rises each ring against its own last look, then adds the rise to the slot the ring feeds. It reads the routes under the publish's lock (`RxShared::routes_settled`), because a publish clears a leaving route before it stores the new one.
- **Changing what a ring counts ripples** (#234, `start_holes`). About 17 tests read a never-written ring and assert `holes`: ring, rx, tx engine ring, stream, status strings, `tests/hot_path.rs`. Find them first with `grep -rn '\.holes\b\|"holes"' crates/dstack`. Assert the exact split (`start_holes: N`), never a sum that passes either way. obs's `rx_holes` is the sum of the rings' `holes` (`obs/plugins/dante-input/ffi/src/status.rs`), so name the change in the CHANGELOG for obs and camera-box.
- **Who writes the rings** (#326, `rx/intake.rs`): the pump in the normal profile (it drains the flow sockets just before each block, `Intake::drain_before_block` with its step now - (P - H) - start since #477/#476; `drain_into_rings(None)` while the clock is not ready), the app's consumer when it pulls (#307, `RxShared::app_intake`, dlink's `RxPull` on an output callback: then nothing writes while it does not pull, and the flow reads silent; its `late` and lost holes are judged against P + the time since its last drain, `intake::reads_apart`), else the RX thread's pass; claimed once at the start (`rx/claim.rs`: `RxShared::pump_intake`, `app_intake`, `Receiver::new`). A counter the writer keeps (packets, late, holes, wake and write delays) is written by the pump's thread under the normal profile: never add one that assumes the RX thread (a per-pass count, its clock). Under the pump the wake and write delays include the wait for the next block; the pump drains only slots with a flow, so the RX thread empties the others (`stray`) at each wake, and a new flow's first drain drops what waited (`stray`): a socket left unread holds seconds of an old flow, read later as late. The RX thread reads the writer's `source` and `heard` (stats atomics) for its keepalives and sends one only to the plan's own transmitter: a plan change to another transmitter seen by the RX thread before the writer sends the old one nothing, while the same transmitter re-granted may get one on the old flow's `heard` for up to a block. A group socket the RX thread replaces closes only after the writer acknowledged a later drain (`rx/handles.rs`); the writer reads a group handle only when its group is the plan's (`intake::pick`). The thread's reads go through its mio sockets: on Windows mio re-arms readiness only on its own read, so a read past mio there leaves the slot never readable again.
- **Stalling the RX thread in a test** (#326): `RxShared::stall_thread(ns)` (doc-hidden), served at the top of its loop. `tests/rx_stall.rs` uses it with real threads; its low-latency case is the control that proves the stall reaches a writer.
- **A new status key** goes into `ctl/status/tests.rs`'s `WORDS` list, or every status test fails on the unknown word.
- **A field on the minute timing line:** keep the message text (`RX flow timing in the last minute: ...`) unchanged. Consumers grep it, so add fields only.
- **A total split by cause** (#281, the pump's `rebootstraps_late/_clock_back/_reanchor/_retime`): keep only the parts as atomics and derive the total as their sum in the view (`PumpStats::view`). A separate total atomic lets a watch look between the two adds see a total without its cause (`cause=none`), and the cause's rise is lost the next second.
- **A figure several readers look at (#342, the mean age, `rx/age.rs`):** keep running totals the writer only adds to (`Ages::record`, two relaxed `fetch_add` in `intake::record_arrivals`) and let each reader diff its own two looks (`RxCounts::lateness_mean_ns_since`); never a window a reader swaps to 0, which a second reader (dasio beside the heartbeat and the status) steals and two swaps tear. Only the heartbeat's `take_max_lateness` keeps per-second state (`Ages::take`). Cap what one packet adds (`AGE_CAP_NS`) so a sum of nonsense stamps cannot wrap in a run.
- **The reported ages are the ARRIVAL's, judged once a slot's read is done** (#350, `intake::record_arrivals`, `age::arrival_age_ns`): `max_lateness` (0x8003), `lateness_peak`, the judge and the mean take each packet's age at the read less its wait (Linux: the kernel stamp's wake; Windows: the bracket of the slot's last read that left its socket empty, `Lane::read_at`, and the newest packet's age at this read), the wait goes to `read_waits`/`read_wait_peak_ns`. `late`, holes and `late_at_read` stay the reader's verdict at the read. A test that pins an age with a synthetic `now` while the kernel stamps real receive times must `set_stamps(false)` (the Windows path; a same-`now` read before gives the age at the read): stamped, the arrival is the age less the real ~30 ms `settle()`. A bracket needs a read that EMPTIED the same socket (`Lane::read_at` keeps its handle): a read capped at `DRAIN`/`PUMP_DRAIN`, a read error, a flow change and a move to the group socket clear it. Without a stamp a late burst just before a read reads as on time: `lateness_at_read_peak` keeps the upper bound.
- **Positions across a time-base change** (#379, stream-obs: a 43.9-day re-anchor back): never keep a sample position only by `fetch_max`/`max` (the ring's furthest read, a flow's furthest end). Writer and reader both jump with a new PTP base, and a max kept on the old base refuses every later write as late for the whole step. Judge a jump of a whole ring (`ring::went_back`, `SLOTS`) as a new base and start over; within one ring keep the old rule. Test with the field's delta (`to_samples(3792840060134767, 96000)`), a step past the test's lead but within a ring, and a stray old-base packet after the step.
- **Another base either way** (#489, strih-snv 25.11.2026: a re-granted flow's first 764 packets ~8.6 days BEHIND our clock, then on it, read `gap_frames` 71074092504): #379's rule judged only a base AHEAD. `holes::apart(pos, now)` (a ring or more either way) keeps a packet out of the holes, ages, network latency and `wire_late` (`intake::place` returns `None`; `late` still counts). A flow end a ring or more BEHIND the clock is another base's only when the clock passed it by a ring MORE than the reader's own clock moved since the end moved (`Lane::end_mono`, `follow_clock`'s `since`): positions alone cannot tell our clock's forward step from a silence on our base, and a silence's hole is a real loss. Read a field "loss" against `rx_late` and `rx_written_late` of the same status line (764 x fpp 16 x 2 ch = 24448): late packets of a new flow with one giant gap are this. A test packet past a ring from now measures nothing: keep a "very late on our base" test within a ring (`socket/tests.rs` wire-late at 0.5 s).
- **Unplaced packets restart a flow's sequence** (#447, `intake::receive`): a packet read with no time (an app's idle drain, `RxPull::drain_idle`'s `drain_into_rings(None)`; a clock not ready) is counted `unplaced` and marks the lane (`Lane::restart`); `place` then runs `holes::follow_clock` on the old end (a time base gone back meanwhile still loses the old holes, #379) and starts the sequence again, or the next placed packet opens a hole across everything that came and it expires as a false gap. A hole open before the run stays (if its filler came unplaced it still reads lost: a residual, unseen at a 250 ms drain). Before calling a field "never reached our socket" a loss, compare `gap_frames` with fpp x the `unplaced` rise (dsg 19.11.2026: 7154848 = 32 x 223589 exactly) and the device status's `packets` and `keepalives` rates (3000/s and 4/s at 96 kHz fpp 32 mean the flow ran whole), and the sender's own log (`TX flow stopped by its receiver` / `expired`). dasio's `rx_arrival` line reads placed packets only: "no packet in the last second" during an idle drain is the same artifact.
- **`late` is judged against the position the reader reads next, never against now** (#477, `intake::reads_apart`): the pump reads by its schedule, so a wake late by `behind` reads first the block due that long ago, and its drain (`Intake::drain_before_block`) passes the step now - (P - H) - start (B on time, B + behind late; H the hand-off lead of a leading pump, `Timing::read_point`, #476: against P a leading pump counted on-time packets late, `stream/tests/drain.rs::a_leading_pump_judges_late_against_the_block_it_reads_at_p_less_h`); the app's pull passes the time since its last drain (#307), the RX thread the plan's block. A rule on now - P - B counted every packet that waited in the socket through a late wake (presenter-listen 21.11.2026: P 5 ms, wakes 4-6 ms late, 40 "late" with 0.3 ms arrivals, `late_at_read` 0). Before calling a field `late` (0x8004) real, read the pump watch line of that second (`behind_max_us` against P) and `late_at_read`: late with `late_at_read` 0 and a wake about P late was this. A whole-device proof is `dlink/tests/pump_late_count.rs` (a 9 ms nap every 256 blocks at P 5 ms, W 20 ms: before the fix 3-6 late a second from the flow's first second); the sender advertises P 5 ms too, or the flow's latency is the sender's larger default (#352) and nothing reads late.
- **`RxShared::publish` runs inside a no-allocation window** (#464): `tests/hot_path.rs::the_pump_s_drain_of_the_rx_flows_does_not_allocate` publishes a new plan between the pump's drains under `assert_no_alloc`. Anything added to the publish must not allocate for an unchanged result: work in a fixed `[_; MAX_FLOWS]` (the destination pairs, `rx/destinations.rs` `Pairs`) and allocate only on a change (`RxDestinations::update` takes a slice). A per-publish `Vec` failed it with 2 allocations. Log a change after its lock is dropped.
- **The RX destination pairs** (#464, `RxShared::rx_destinations`, `Media`/`Link::rx_destinations`): a multicast flow's group and port, our address with a unicast flow's slot port, for dasio's filter match table; the generation moves only when the set changes. A device subscribed to ITSELF never takes a multicast flow (`subscribe::resolve`'s own-records resolver answers `MulticastLookup::None`), so a whole-device multicast test needs a second device (`tests/embed.rs`: another name, ARC 4441, CMC 8801, flows 4456, its own process id) subscribed to the first's TX channel that a saved `[[multicast]]` flow carries.
